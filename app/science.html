04 / Quantitative models

Formulas

Equations reflect the implemented screening models. Inputs are configurable where noted; numerical sampling and physical assumptions limit interpretation.

Swipe to browse, or use the arrows and keyboard ← → keys.

01 / 10

Local lunar frame

Local lunar basis and observer position

At latitude φ and longitude λ, define local up, east, and north. The observer sits at reference lunar radius plus terrain elevation and mast height.

Apparent body geometry

Azimuth, elevation, and apparent angular radius

v is the observer-to-body vector, d its distance, and rbody the body radius. Geometry uses SPICE light-time plus stellar aberration (LT+S).

Sampled terrain horizon

Elevation angle from observer to terrain sample

The horizon at each bearing is the maximum sampled elevation. Radial spacing is 5 m to 2 km, 20 m to 5 km, then 80 m to 250 km. Missing terrain stays unknown.

Solar disk overlap

Apparent disk intersection and estimated direct-sun fraction

For separation d and apparent radii r1, r2, the overlap gives the blocked solar fraction. Terrain visibility uses 512 deterministic disk samples; the joint nominal fraction is a product approximation.

Solar array power

Solar flux, panel incidence, and direct illumination

A, efficiency η, derating δ, panel orientation, and direct-sun fraction come from the study configuration. Tracking arrays use unit incidence.

Battery energy

Sampled battery energy balance with charge and discharge efficiencies

Capacity C bounds the stored energy. Activity start and end times split intervals exactly; generation is held constant between samples.

Radio link budget

Wavelength, antenna gain, free-space loss, and link margin

This simplified free-space budget uses editable hardware, rate, losses, and margin assumptions. A positive margin does not guarantee geometric access or DSN availability.

Terrain slope & route cost

Plane-fit slope, residual roughness, and path cost

The site screen fits a plane to native DEM pixels. Eight-neighbor A* routing excludes unknown, over-slope, and prohibited cells, then accounts for uphill effort and turns.

Ideal hopper screen

Ideal propulsive delta-v and flat-surface ballistic hop

Vacuum, impulsive hop under constant lunar gravity. This is a screening estimate, not a trajectory or a model of a specific vehicle.

Circular reference orbit

Circular two-body orbit radius, angular rate, and phase

The user-defined reference orbit uses altitude, initial phase, inclination, and RAAN. It is distinct from the NASA LRO definitive-trajectory example.

05 / How results are produced

Methodology

LUNA is a research-planning and education aid. Results screen candidate conditions; they are not landing clearance, guaranteed communications, or flight-qualified predictions.

  1. 01

    Load and disclose inputs

    NASA LOLA raster cells are sampled in their lunar frame; NASA NAIF kernels provide ephemerides, orientation, time conversion, and station locations. The study export records inputs, source manifest, kernel metadata, and code hashes.

  2. 02

    Compute time-dependent geometry

    UTC is converted to SPICE ET. Local Sun, Earth, LRO, and station geometry is evaluated in supported frames with LT+S where used. Surface visibility accounts for the local terrain horizon; contact windows are found from sampled sign changes and refined by bisection.

  3. 03

    Sample uncertainty and visibility

    Terrain-height perturbations create deterministic sensitivity bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. Sunlight samples the apparent solar disk; the joint eclipse/terrain nominal fraction is a product approximation. Unknown terrain remains marked unknown.

  4. 04

    Evaluate power, contact, and candidate schedules

    Solar production and overlapping activity loads feed a bounded battery model. DSN visibility requires both lunar antenna elevation and the terrestrial station mask; RF margin is evaluated separately. Candidate activity starts are sample-aligned and recomputed against conservative state of charge.

  5. 05

    Screen terrain and routes

    Site04 footprint cells are fit to a plane for slope and residual roughness screening. Rover routing uses an eight-neighbor A* grid, slope and polygon constraints, uphill-effort cost, and turn penalties. Finite grid resolution can miss local hazards.

Important model limits

The globe display is a textured sphere, not a terrain-displaced mesh. Horizon results depend on finite DEM coverage and sampling; the far-field +15 km limit is an explicit assumed height. The Sun is uniform, Earth spherical, and neither atmospheric refraction nor limb darkening is modeled. No DSN reservation, weather/interference model, detailed thermal/battery-management model, rock-scale hazard map, landing dynamics, or mission hardware validation is included.
