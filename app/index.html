The Mission

Sunlight doesn’t
guarantee a signal.

At the lunar south pole, surrounding terrain can block sunlight and direct communication with Earth. A promising landing site may offer favorable conditions at one moment and very different conditions at another.

Where you land, and when, changes everything.

Behind the Interface

Built around
lunar geometry.

LUNA uses lunar terrain information and celestial position calculations to visualize how the local horizon affects sunlight and Earth visibility.

Lunar terrain

Terrain elevation helps establish the local horizon.

Celestial positions

Sun and Earth positions are evaluated for selected locations and times.

Visibility analysis

Apparent positions are compared with the horizon to screen potential visibility windows.

Data, methodology and limits +

LUNA uses NASA JPL’s SPICE toolkit, DE440s translations, and the MOON_ME lunar frame. Where elevation data are enabled and available, LUNA samples the surrounding terrain to estimate a local horizon. Regional elevation and a higher-resolution Site04 model come from the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter.

Visibility windows depend on the settings, terrain coverage, and time sampling. Small or short-lived obstructions can be missed. Earth visibility indicates a geometric opportunity for contact. Assessing an RF link or a landing requires additional evidence.

About the Project

Created to make lunar exploration more accessible.

I’m Artem Kachan, a student and software developer from Ukraine. I created LUNA to make complex lunar exploration concepts easier to visualize, compare, and understand.

LUNA is a hackathon project focused on understanding illumination and communication constraints at candidate lunar south pole landing sites.
